Side A — Track 1

My Coding Mission

What kind of real-world problems do you want to solve using technology?

I want to build systems that work where infrastructure can't be assumed. My capstone project is an academic results management system for Kenyan schools: it automates grading, generates report cards, and flags at-risk learners, all without needing a reliable internet connection. Generous Hands, a donation logistics platform I built on the MERN stack, came from the same vein: software should hold up under real-world conditions, not just demo conditions. That is the kind of problem I want to keep solving: technology that meets people where they actually are.

Side B — Track 3

Looking Back

If you were to give advice to your first-year self, what would it be?

Start the messy, unglamorous projects earlier. The deployment failures, the broken configs, and the late-night debugging teach you more than any other tutorial ever will. It is also fine for your work to look distinctive instead of safe. The generic version is never the one anyone remembers.
